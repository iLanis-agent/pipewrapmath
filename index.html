Pipe wrap math

A freeze goes wrong in three places: the foam bought by the handful instead of by the meter of exposed pipe, the heat tape plugged in without anyone asking what it draws, and the drip left running with no idea whether the bucket or the tub is the honest mental picture. Pipe wrap math turns the freeze into the wrap, turns the pipe into the tape, and turns the drip into the liters. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Wrap plan - the meters of exposed pipe at a labeled tube length become the foam tubes to buy.
  • Tape plan - the meters of pipe at a labeled watts-per-meter become the watts the heat tape draws.
  • Drip plan - the dripping faucets and the hours at a labeled drip rate become the liters let through.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 1 m foam tubes, 7 W of heat tape per meter and a 0.18 L-an-hour drip per faucet - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. Real freeze protection is draining the line and shutting the valve: insulation buys time, not immunity, and heat tape follows its manufacturer's instructions exactly. This is planning arithmetic, not freeze insurance.
